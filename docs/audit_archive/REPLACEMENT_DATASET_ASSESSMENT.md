# TruthScan Replacement-Dataset Assessment

**Research date:** 2026-10-26  
**Scope:** public documentation only; no dataset files, rows, model weights, or acquisition scripts were accessed.  
**Decision status:** `CANDIDATE_RECOMMENDED_FOR_OWNER_REVIEW`

## Executive recommendation

Use a staged plan. Preserve the existing Track A article-level Fake/Real interface, but do not select a replacement or rebuild inference yet. The current local dataset and historical artifacts remain blocked by the repository's existing provenance, rights, and trust-root findings.

For the fastest responsible academic path, submit **FEVER** for owner review as a separately named claim-verification track. FEVER has the clearest primary documentation among the candidates reviewed: it defines 185,445 claims, the three verification labels, evidence fields, and a specific license notice tied to applicable Wikipedia terms. It is not an article-level Fake/Real dataset, and its terms do not automatically authorize public hosting, commercial use, model-weight distribution, or redistribution of transformed data.

For the existing article-level product, **NELA-GT is the strongest technical research lead**, not an approved replacement. Its official documentation provides article fields and outlet-level labels and explains a copyright-aware text transformation, but it does not establish a broad downstream license. Its labels describe source reliability, not the truth of each article. No article-level candidate is currently justified for acquisition or public inference.

## Evidence and limitations by candidate

### FEVER

**Verified facts.** The official dataset page describes 185,445 claims generated by altering Wikipedia sentences, three labels, and JSONL fields including claim and evidence references. The original paper describes the same task and reports that evidence was recorded for the first two labels. The official license page states that annotations incorporate Wikipedia material and must be used under the applicable Wikipedia article terms or CC BY-SA 3.0 where article terms are unavailable.

**Inference and limitation.** FEVER is the closest documented lead for Direction B because its contract matches the proposed `SUPPORTED`/`REFUTED`/`INSUFFICIENT_EVIDENCE` separation. It cannot be relabeled as `REAL`/`FAKE` without changing the task. A future public demo would need a documented evidence-retrieval and display policy, a fixed source snapshot or permitted retrieval method, attribution, privacy review, and a separate review of model and derived-data distribution.

### NELA-GT

**Verified facts.** The official repository documents multiple dated releases, SQLite/JSON formats, article and source metadata, embedded tweets, and source-level aggregated labels. It also states that source articles may be copyrighted and describes token masking of article text for analysis use.

**Inference and limitation.** NELA-GT could support an article/source-credibility research question, but source identity can become a shortcut and outlet-level labels should not be treated as individual article truth. A leakage-resistant study would require source-grouped splits, duplicate/near-duplicate checks, temporal holdouts, and a clearly defined target. The published transformation does not resolve copyright, model distribution, public hosting, privacy, deletion, or takedown questions.

### LIAR

**Verified facts.** The UCSB archive exposes a dated LIAR archive, and the ACL paper identifies a decade-long collection of approximately 12.8K manually labeled short statements from PolitiFact, with contextual metadata and six labels.

**Inference and limitation.** LIAR could support a claim-level academic experiment, but a binary collapse would be a new label policy and would discard the original semantics. The sources reviewed do not establish permission for all intended downstream uses. It is not a defensible replacement for TruthScan's full-article Fake/Real contract without a separately approved task redesign.

### Fakeddit

**Verified facts.** The official repository and paper describe Reddit submissions, text, images, metadata, comments, and 2-way/3-way/6-way classification. The paper reports over one million samples and distant supervision; the repository states that released components and usage instructions differ for text/metadata, images, and comments.

**Inference and limitation.** This would require a multimodal or deliberately text-only redesign, component-specific rights review, Reddit/platform and user-content considerations, and leakage controls for users, subreddits, domains, and duplicate submissions. The available public materials do not establish broad public-hosting or redistribution permission for every component.

### Zenodo v1.1

**Verified facts.** The official record identifies v1.1, creator Sadam Hussain, publication date January 2, 2026, DOI `10.5281/zenodo.18135595`, separate code and dataset archives, and a displayed MIT code / CC BY 4.0 dataset declaration.

**Inference and limitation.** The displayed package license is evidence about the record's stated terms, not proof that the creator owns or can sublicense every article, image, or other third-party item. Task 40–41 already left both college and public-website gates blocked. This candidate should remain blocked unless written clarification and a source-to-file/provenance package are supplied and reviewed.

## Direction comparison

### Direction A — article-level Fake/Real classification

This direction preserves the current API and user-facing Fake/Real contract. NELA-GT is the only reviewed lead with a clearly article-shaped structure, but its target is source reliability and its text is transformed. Fakeddit is larger but multimodal and distantly supervised. Zenodo v1.1 is closer in its record description but remains rights-blocked. LIAR and FEVER do not provide the same unit of analysis.

Required work would include a new dataset identity and rights record, an owner-approved label policy, duplicate/source/temporal leakage controls, a reproducible split manifest, a clean candidate-build environment, and independently trusted artifact release metadata. A benchmark score would not establish real-world fact-checking reliability.

### Direction B — evidence-based claim verification

FEVER is the strongest research lead for this direction. The Track B contract should remain `SUPPORTED`, `REFUTED`, and `INSUFFICIENT_EVIDENCE`, with evidence treated as part of the result rather than an optional explanation. The prototype must not be presented as a Fake/Real classifier or as a live fact-checking service unless it actually retrieves permitted evidence and passes separate evaluation.

This direction has a cleaner semantic fit for the existing isolated prototype, but it is a new product track. It still requires owner authorization, applicable-source licensing review, evidence snapshot/retrieval design, evaluation, privacy/security review, and explicit public-hosting approval.

## Qualitative implementation effort

- **FEVER / Direction B:** moderate-to-high. The contract and benchmark are documented, but evidence-aware retrieval, source-version handling, attribution, evaluation, and a separate UI/API are required.
- **NELA-GT / Direction A research:** high. The data is large, source-labeled, transformed, and likely requires source-grouped and time-aware evaluation plus rights clarification.
- **LIAR:** moderate for an academic claim classifier, high for adapting it to the current article interface because of label and unit mismatch.
- **Fakeddit:** high because of multimodal inputs, component-specific rights, large-scale preprocessing, and leakage controls.
- **Zenodo v1.1:** technically uncertain and rights-blocked; effort cannot be responsibly estimated beyond the need for a written rights/provenance clarification package.

These are scope assessments, not schedule or cost promises.

## Owner decisions still required

1. Whether to keep the existing Track A dataset blocked and authorize a named replacement **assessment** only.
2. Whether to pursue FEVER as a separate academic Track B research lead, without calling it Fake/Real.
3. Whether intended use includes internal academic evaluation, public inference, commercial use, trained-model distribution, or raw/transformed-data redistribution.
4. Who is accountable for rights review and what evidence standard is required before acquisition.
5. Whether public hosting is in scope now; if yes, approve a separate deployment/security/privacy gate.
6. Whether the project should abandon article-level replacement research until a candidate supplies defensible article-truth labels and downstream-use terms.

No choice above is preselected. Owner authorization to assess a candidate would not authorize downloading, processing, training, artifact creation, hosting, or deployment.

## Recommended next smallest action

The owner should record one explicit decision: **authorize or decline a rights-and-suitability assessment of FEVER for a separately named academic claim-verification demonstration**. If authorized, the next gated work should be a written evidence checklist for the exact FEVER revision, applicable Wikipedia terms, source snapshot, intended display/distribution scope, and evaluation protocol. It should not touch the existing dataset or production Track A.

If the owner instead requires a binary article-level replacement, the next action is to nominate one candidate—preferably NELA-GT or the Zenodo lead only for further review—and obtain a candidate-specific provenance and rights package before any acquisition.
