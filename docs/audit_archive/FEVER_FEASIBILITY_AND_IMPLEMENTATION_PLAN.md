# FEVER Feasibility and Implementation Plan for TruthScan

**Research date:** 2026-10-26  
**Scope:** static code review and public-source verification only. No FEVER files, rows, models, packages, or inference paths were accessed or executed.  
**Status:** `TECHNICALLY_FEASIBLE_WITH_MAJOR_GAPS`

## Executive recommendation

FEVER is a defensible **research lead** for a separately named academic claim-verification track. It is not a replacement for TruthScan's existing article-level Fake/Real classifier and should not be connected to Track A's prediction route or UI.

The smallest meaningful first release should be an owner-approved, local-only claim-verification demonstration with:

- the existing isolated claim request and three-outcome contract;
- a fixed, explicitly permitted evidence source or snapshot;
- traceable evidence references and source links;
- an abstention result when evidence is missing, contradictory, stale, or unavailable;
- synthetic fixtures and contract tests before any FEVER acquisition or model work.

This plan does not authorize acquisition, training, evaluation, public hosting, or deployment.

## Part A — FEVER facts and rights assessment

### Verified from official sources

The official FEVER page describes 185,445 claims generated by altering sentences extracted from Wikipedia. It defines `SUPPORTS`, `REFUTES`, and `NOT ENOUGH INFO`, and records evidence sentence references for the first two categories. Training/development records are JSONL with claim, label, and evidence fields; `NOT ENOUGH INFO` uses null evidence references. The original NAACL paper describes the same task and reports annotator agreement of 0.6841 Fleiss kappa. The official repository contains annotation-platform, baseline, scorer, and dataset-preparation code.

**Source-consistency limitation:** the current official dataset page states 185,445 claims, while the official repository README states 185,441. This small discrepancy must be resolved by selecting and recording one exact FEVER release before any reproducible acquisition or evaluation; this plan does not assume either figure is the final file count.

Sources:

- [Official FEVER dataset documentation](https://fever.ai/dataset/fever.html)
- [Official FEVER license notice](https://fever.ai/download/fever/license.html)
- [FEVER original paper](https://aclanthology.org/N18-1074/)
- [Official FEVER repository](https://github.com/awslabs/fever)

### What the license notice actually says

The official notice states that the data annotations incorporate material from Wikipedia, that the applicable Wikipedia Copyright Policy and article-specific terms govern where available, and that CC BY-SA 3.0 applies where Wikipedia license terms are unavailable. It also says the files may not be used except in compliance with those applicable terms.

This supports the following narrow conclusions:

| Question | Evidence-supported status | Reason |
|---|---|---|
| FEVER labels and annotation package exist | `SUPPORTED_BY_VERIFIED_EVIDENCE` | The official page and paper define the labels, fields, and annotation task. |
| Wikipedia-derived material is covered by one uniform blanket license | `UNRESOLVED` | The notice points to applicable article-specific terms and only uses CC BY-SA 3.0 where article terms are unavailable. |
| Attribution | `CONDITIONAL_PENDING_CONFIRMATION` | Attribution is required by CC BY-SA 3.0 and may be required by applicable article terms; the exact notice and source attribution scheme must be fixed for the selected material. |
| Share-alike | `CONDITIONAL_PENDING_CONFIRMATION` | It follows where CC BY-SA 3.0 or an equivalent applicable article term governs; the notice does not establish one uniform share-alike rule for every item. |
| Training and internal evaluation | `CONDITIONAL_PENDING_CONFIRMATION` | These uses may be possible under the applicable source terms, but the project must identify the exact revision and terms before proceeding. |
| Derived dataset distribution | `UNRESOLVED` | The notice does not provide a single project-wide answer for all derived material; source-specific terms and the proposed contents must be reviewed. |
| Model-weight distribution | `UNRESOLVED` | The official FEVER notice does not expressly grant or prohibit distribution of weights trained on the material. Owner/legal review is required for the exact training and release design. |
| Public inference hosting | `UNRESOLVED` | Hosting a service and displaying evidence introduce additional source, attribution, privacy, and operational questions not answered by the dataset page. |
| Commercial use | `UNRESOLVED` | No blanket commercial-use permission was established for all incorporated Wikipedia material. |
| Citation and source notices | `SUPPORTED_BY_VERIFIED_EVIDENCE` for citing FEVER; `CONDITIONAL_PENDING_CONFIRMATION` for source-specific notices | The official page provides a citation, while source/article notices must follow the applicable terms. |

These are evidence classifications, not legal opinions. A qualified rights reviewer must determine whether the planned activity is permitted.

### Important scope distinctions

1. **Annotations and labels are not automatically equivalent to all source content.** The official notice describes annotations that incorporate Wikipedia material; it does not prove that every claim, evidence sentence, page extract, and metadata field has identical rights.
2. **Evidence sentences are retained source material.** FEVER's evidence references point to Wikipedia pages and sentence IDs. A future system must identify whether it stores, redisplays, caches, or only links to those sentences.
3. **A model is not automatically cleared because the training data is public.** Training, weight distribution, hosted inference, and raw or transformed-data redistribution must be reviewed separately.
4. **FEVER is not live fact-checking.** The benchmark is based on a defined Wikipedia snapshot and its labels do not establish current truth for every future claim.

## Part B — Static assessment of the existing implementation

### What exists

The isolated prototype at `evaluation_results/new_release_candidate_2026-10-22/task44_synthetic_prototype/` contains:

- `synthetic_claim_api.py`: strict request/response validation, the three semantic outcomes, synthetic fixture generation, controlled errors, HTML escaping, and an explicit prohibition on Track A conversion.
- `test_synthetic_claim_api.py`: tests for all outcomes, empty/oversized claims, malformed evidence, missing evidence, hostile strings, invalid responses, Track A conversion, and production/default-mode refusal.
- `UI_MOCKUP.html`: a disabled static page marked synthetic-only, with an `INSUFFICIENT_EVIDENCE` example and a Track A separation disclaimer.
- `TASK44_SYNTHETIC_API_CONTRACT.md`: the documented claim request and response contract.

### What does not exist

Static inspection establishes that the prototype:

- does not retrieve evidence;
- does not call Wikipedia or another knowledge source;
- does not load a FEVER model or any artifact;
- does not expose a production route;
- returns pre-authored outcomes based on synthetic claim prefixes;
- displays synthetic placeholders rather than traceable real sources;
- cannot establish whether a real claim is supported or refuted.

Therefore the current prototype is a safe contract demonstration, not a FEVER implementation and not an operational fact-checker.

### Reusable components

The following can be reused after separate approval:

- request validation and length limits in `synthetic_claim_api.py`;
- the exact outcome vocabulary and abstention behavior;
- response validation requiring evidence for `SUPPORTED` and `REFUTED`;
- controlled error rendering and hostile-string escaping;
- the synthetic contract tests as unit-test patterns;
- the existing Track A frontend only as a separate application shell, not as a verdict display;
- repository CI patterns for synthetic tests, once dependency reproducibility is resolved.

### Required code changes, not yet implemented

- Add a separate Track B service boundary, disabled by default and not imported by Track A routes.
- Replace fixture selection with an evidence-retrieval interface that accepts a permitted source adapter.
- Add stable evidence identifiers, source title/URL, snapshot or retrieval timestamp, and excerpt boundaries.
- Add an adjudication layer that returns a verdict only when evidence and model confidence meet a documented policy; otherwise return `INSUFFICIENT_EVIDENCE`.
- Add a contradiction policy so conflicting evidence produces abstention or an explicitly labeled conflict state, not an invented refutation.
- Add source-attribution and license-notice handling to the response and UI.
- Add independent artifact and dependency verification for any real model.
- Add evaluation code for both verdict labels and evidence retrieval quality.

## Part C — Minimum credible implementation

### Proposed architecture

```text
User claim
   |
   v
Track B request validator
   |
   v
Permitted source adapter  ---> source/version/license metadata
   |
   v
Evidence retriever
   |
   v
Evidence validator -------> missing/contradictory/stale => INSUFFICIENT_EVIDENCE
   |
   v
Claim-evidence adjudicator
   |
   +--> SUPPORTED
   +--> REFUTED
   +--> INSUFFICIENT_EVIDENCE
   |
   v
Separate claim-verification UI with evidence links and disclaimer

Track A Fake/Real API and UI remain outside this flow.
```

The first source adapter should be a fixed, explicitly permitted snapshot or a source whose terms clearly allow the intended access and display pattern. Live web retrieval should be deferred until freshness, rate limits, privacy, takedown, attribution, and operational security are approved.

### Stage 0 — Approval and scope gate

**Dependency:** owner decision; qualified rights review where needed.

**Work:** record the FEVER revision, intended academic/noncommercial scope, whether evidence excerpts are stored or only linked, whether model weights may be distributed, and whether any public hosting is in scope.

**Acceptance criteria:** written owner authorization for assessment; rights checklist identifies applicable Wikipedia terms; no acquisition starts until the checklist is accepted.

**Estimated engineering time:** 1–2 agent-hours, excluding owner/legal waiting time.

### Stage 1 — Contract-only Track B foundation

**Dependency:** none beyond the current synthetic prototype.

**Work:** preserve and document the separate route/service boundary, request validation, three outcomes, abstention semantics, source-reference schema, and disabled-by-default configuration.

**Acceptance criteria:** synthetic tests pass; no Track A import or route; malformed and unsupported evidence always fail closed; UI never shows Fake/Real labels.

**Estimated engineering time:** 2–4 agent-hours.

### Stage 2 — Permitted evidence-source adapter

**Dependency:** Stage 0 rights scope and an approved source/snapshot.

**Work:** implement an interface for source lookup, stable page/sentence identifiers, source URLs, retrieval/snapshot metadata, and attribution notices. Keep the adapter replaceable.

**Acceptance criteria:** synthetic adapter tests cover successful lookup, no result, stale source, malformed source data, source failure, and attribution output. No live network calls occur in unit tests.

**Estimated engineering time:** 4–8 agent-hours after the source is approved.

### Stage 3 — Evidence-aware adjudication and abstention

**Dependency:** Stage 2 and an approved model or deterministic baseline.

**Work:** combine claim and evidence inputs; enforce evidence requirements; return confidence/uncertainty only as model signals; abstain on missing, contradictory, low-confidence, or out-of-scope evidence.

**Acceptance criteria:** no supported/refuted result without valid evidence; contradiction and service-failure fixtures return `INSUFFICIENT_EVIDENCE`; no conversion to Track A labels.

**Estimated engineering time:** 4–8 agent-hours, excluding model creation or approval.

### Stage 4 — Separate UI and explanation layer

**Dependency:** Stage 3 response schema.

**Work:** show the claim, outcome, evidence links/excerpts, source timestamp or snapshot, limitations, and a persistent disclaimer. Keep the Track B page visually and semantically separate from the article classifier.

**Acceptance criteria:** hostile source text is inert; missing evidence is visible; source attribution is present; no “real/fake” wording appears on the claim page; loading and error recovery work.

**Estimated engineering time:** 3–6 agent-hours.

### Stage 5 — Reproducible evaluation and release review

**Dependency:** approved data revision, source snapshot, model artifact, dependency environment, and evaluation protocol.

**Work:** measure verdict quality and evidence retrieval quality separately; report abstention/coverage, label metrics, evidence precision/recall or task-appropriate retrieval metrics, source leakage, temporal drift, and failure cases. Bind model artifacts to an independently trusted manifest.

**Acceptance criteria:** exact data/source revision and split manifest are recorded; tests and evaluation reproduce in a clean target environment; artifact integrity and trust-root checks pass; owner approves the exact use scope.

**Estimated engineering time:** 8–16 agent-hours after all inputs are authorized and available. Training or model acquisition is excluded from this estimate and requires separate authorization.

### Estimate summary

The engineering work for the minimum credible Track B slice is approximately **22–44 agent-hours**, excluding acquisition, model training, package installation, rights review, and owner waiting time. The main uncertainty is not coding; it is the approval and evidence needed for the selected source, evidence display, model artifacts, and intended distribution.

## Test and evidence strategy

### Contract tests

- valid claims and all three outcomes;
- empty, whitespace-only, oversized, and malformed claims;
- missing evidence for `SUPPORTED`/`REFUTED`;
- empty evidence for `INSUFFICIENT_EVIDENCE`;
- malformed evidence identifiers, snippets, URLs, and timestamps;
- contradictory evidence;
- unknown outcomes and extra response fields;
- source timeout/unavailability and malformed source responses;
- hostile text rendered as inert text;
- explicit refusal to convert Track B outcomes to Fake/Real;
- production configuration refusing to enable Track B without approval.

### Evaluation evidence

The eventual evaluation record must separately report:

- verdict classification quality;
- evidence retrieval quality and evidence sufficiency;
- abstention coverage and error rates;
- source/date distribution and leakage controls;
- performance by claim type and source availability;
- freshness limits relative to the fixed source snapshot;
- exact data, source, model, dependency, and split identities.

Benchmark results must not be presented as proof of real-world fact-checking accuracy.

## Unresolved approvals and blockers

- Owner authorization for a FEVER-specific assessment is not yet recorded.

- Applicable Wikipedia article terms must be reviewed for the specific evidence material displayed, stored, or redistributed.

- No approved real model artifact or independently trusted release root exists for Track B.
- Dependency reproducibility remains a project-level blocker for a clean candidate build.
- The current Track A dataset and historical artifacts remain blocked and are outside this plan.
