# Task 42 — Recommendation and Evidence Gaps

## Recommendation

Do not select a dataset for the current TruthScan public website. Keep the public track blocked.

For a college demonstration, the owner may consider authorizing a **separate FEVER claim-verification demonstration**, not a TruthScan fake/real model. FEVER has the clearest official license notice among the reviewed alternatives, but its Wikipedia-derived terms and task semantics must be reviewed before acquisition or processing. If the demonstration must remain article-level fake-versus-real, no reviewed candidate has sufficient evidence yet.

NELA-GT is a technically interesting article-level research lead because its official project documents source-level labels and copyright-aware text transformation. Its lack of a verified broad downstream license keeps it blocked. Zenodo v1.1 remains subject to the Task 40–41 rights request. LIAR and WELFake remain blocked for the reasons recorded in the comparison.

## Required evidence before any candidate acquisition

- Owner-authorized candidate and intended use.
- Exact version, source identifier, acquisition date, and source-reported metadata.
- Independently retained local digest after authorized acquisition.
- License/terms covering the exact files and underlying material.
- Rights-holder authority, third-party content exclusions, privacy and takedown terms.
- Permission for training, artifact distribution, hosting, and commercial use separately.
- Approved task, label, grouping, partition, environment, and secure-preflight records.

## Important distinctions

FEVER’s license notice is stronger than a bare repository label, but its permission varies by applicable Wikipedia article terms. NELA-GT’s text transformation reduces ordinary news-consumption risk but is not a license. A dataset compilation license never automatically proves rights in embedded articles, tweets, images, or personal data.
